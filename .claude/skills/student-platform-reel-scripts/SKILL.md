# Student Platform Reel Scripts

Write scripts for 15-20 second Instagram Reels / Facebook videos that promote a
student-learning website or platform. The target feeling: a friend grabbed your
arm and said "wait, you HAVE to know about this," not an ad that interrupted
your scroll.

## Step 1: Get the website data

Before writing anything, you need to actually know the platform — its real
features, its actual value proposition, what makes it different. If the user
hasn't given you this (pasted text, a file, a URL, or prior context in the
conversation), ask for it or look for it in the repo/project before writing.
A script built on guessed or generic features will read as generic — the
whole point of this skill is specificity dressed up as casual talk.

While reading the source material, pull out:
- The single biggest pain point this platform removes (this becomes the Hook)
- 1-3 concrete features or moments of relief (raw material for the Pivot)
- Anything that's actually unique or surprising about it — a specific number,
  a specific feature name, a specific "wait, it does THAT?" moment. Specificity
  is what separates "this app helps you study" from a script that sounds real.

If asked for multiple scripts, pick a *different* pain point or feature angle
for each one rather than rephrasing the same script three times.

## Step 2: Write like you're talking, not typing

The reader should be able to hear this in a real voice. The test: if you can
picture it in a school-ad commercial or a LinkedIn post, it's wrong — rewrite it.

**Never use:** "Hey guys!", "Check out this amazing website!", "Are you a
student struggling with...", "Click the link below!", "game changer",
"you won't believe", or any other line that sounds copy-pasted from a
marketing template. If a sentence could be read in a movie-trailer-announcer
voice, cut it.

**Do use:** contractions, trailing-off thoughts, self-interruptions, filler
that real people say ("like," "I swear," "no but actually"), and specific
concrete details instead of vague claims ("finished my whole reading list
before the bus came" beats "saves you time").

**The voice is a girlfriend, not a narrator.** She's not informing you of a
product — she's mid-conversation, already annoyed at something, and she just
remembered the thing that fixed it for her. Empathy and shared frustration
come first ("we've all sat there at 2am rereading the same paragraph"); the
platform is what she stumbled into, not what she's selling.

**Humor is situational, not jokes bolted on.** Get the humor from the actual
absurdity of studying/procrastinating/the academic system — never punch down,
never make the viewer feel dumb for struggling. The butt of the joke is the
situation ("my highlighter collection has more organization than my notes
ever did"), never the viewer.

**Language:** default to casual spoken Hebrew — the way someone actually
talks, not textbook Hebrew. Avoid formal/literary vocabulary, avoid the kind
of sentence structure that only shows up in writing. If the user asks for a
different language, apply the same rule in that language: whatever a real
person casually saying this out loud would actually say, slang and all.
See `references/examples.md` for full Hebrew example scripts in this voice.

## Step 3: Hit the structure and the clock

Total length: **15-20 seconds, roughly 40-50 words.** This is tight — every
line has to earn its place. Cut anything that's scene-setting or throat-clearing.

| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0-3s | Drop straight into a relatable pain point, a jarring truth, or a mid-thought moment. No greeting, no intro — the video should feel like the camera caught the middle of something. |
| **Pivot / Discovery** | 3-12s | Casually reveal how the platform solved it. Center the *feeling of relief*, not the feature list ("then I found this thing and I swear I stopped panicking two days before exams" beats "it has a flashcard feature"). Weave in the 1-2 most specific/real details from Step 1 here, mentioned in passing like you'd mention it to a friend — never as a bullet-pointed pitch. |
| **CTA / Outro** | 12-20s | A low-pressure, almost throwaway close. Not "go buy now" — more like "just go look it up, thank me later" or a funny parting line that lands the tone rather than pushing a sale. |

## Step 4: Format the output

Give the script as spoken lines with bracketed direction notes woven in for
delivery, facial expression, or on-screen text — these help whoever films it,
so keep them short and actionable, e.g. `[eye-roll]`, `[leans into camera]`,
`[text overlay: 2am, night before the exam]`. Don't over-direct; a note every
line or two is enough.

Use this shape (adapt language/labels to match the user's requested language,
don't leave English scaffolding in a Hebrew script):

```
[opening visual note]
(Hook line — 0-3s)

[visual/expression note]
(Pivot line(s) — 3-12s)

[visual/expression note]
(CTA line — 12-20s)
```

If the user asked for multiple variants, give each one this same shape, and
make sure each leads with a different hook/angle so they're not just
reshuffled sentences.

## Step 5: Self-check before delivering

Read the script back and ask:
- Would I actually say this out loud to my best friend, in these exact words?
- Does the hook work with zero context — does it grab in the first 3 seconds?
- Is the platform mention specific to *this* platform, or could it be swapped
  into an ad for any studying app? If it's swappable, go back to Step 1 and
  pull a more specific detail in.
- Did I stay under ~50 words? Long scripts don't fit 20 seconds — cut, don't
  rush the read.
- Does the CTA feel like a shrug-and-a-smile, not a sales pitch?

If any answer is no, rewrite that beat rather than shipping it as-is.
